# Sample efficiency of LM pretraining: the learning-dynamics view

Sweep date 2026-09-09. arXiv IDs give the month (YYMM). Numbers marked "reports roughly" came through a single page-fetch and should be re-checked in the PDF before quoting.

## 1. Memorization vs generalization dynamics

- **Grokking.** Power et al. 2201.02177 (Jan 2022): on modular-arithmetic tasks, generalization jumps from chance to perfect long after training loss hits zero; smaller data fractions need more optimization. Liu et al. "Omnigrok" 2210.01117 (Oct 2022) reproduce it on non-algorithmic data and tie the delay to weight norm; Nanda et al. 2301.05217 (Jan 2023) show the "jump" hides continuous circuit formation (progress measures). Lv et al. 2506.21551 (Jun 2025) find grokking-like transitions in one-pass OLMoE-7B pretraining: expert-routing pathways move from instance-specific to shared while loss is already flat, and different data groks asynchronously. Morris et al. 2505.24832 (May 2025) put the mechanism in one line: GPT-family models store ~3.6 bits/param; generalization starts when dataset bits exceed capacity, which explains double descent.
- **Exposures to store a fact.** Allen-Zhu & Li 2404.05405 (Apr 2024): 2 bits/param at ~1000 exposures per fact, 1 bit/param at 100 exposures. With a 1:7 useful:junk mix at 100 exposures, useful-knowledge capacity drops 20x; prepending a domain tag (e.g. `wikipedia.org`) shrinks that to 2x. GatedMLP (LLaMA/Mistral) is ~1.3x worse than GPT-2 at 100 exposures. Kandpal et al. 2211.08411 (Nov 2022): QA accuracy is log-linear in the count of pretraining documents mentioning the entities.
- **Acquisition and forgetting curves.** Chang et al. 2406.11813 (Jun 2024), OLMo-1B/7B with injected facts: each exposure adds a small probability increment, then decays as a power law in steps (reports roughly decay exponents 0.14-0.26); duplicated injections learn faster and forget faster than paraphrased; batch 128 vs 2048 shrinks the "learnability threshold" (max interval between exposures that still nets learning) by tens of times; more pretraining tokens do not improve per-exposure acquisition. Tirumala et al. 2205.10770 (May 2022): the forgetting baseline rises with model size. Single exposure: I could not identify a "Tinker" study; the closest is 2608.19168 (Aug 2026), which injects one 194-token passage into GPT-2-124M at step 200 and measures ~0.04 nats of advantage 50 steps later that is undetectable by step 9,536.
- **Diversity / paraphrase.** Allen-Zhu & Li 2309.14316 (Sep 2023): facts seen only in one surface form are memorized but not extractable by QA; paraphrase, shuffling, or translation during pretraining makes them extractable, and extractability correlates with diversity measures. Yang et al. 2409.07431 (Sep 2024, EntiGraph): generic rephrasing saturates fast; entity-graph synthesis scales log-linearly to 455M synthetic tokens. 2609.04180 (Sep 2026, 1B-32B): source-only repetition saturates at ~20 exposures; paraphrase keeps improving to ~40; paraphrase helps at batch 64-96 and not at 1024; at fixed token budget, moving tokens from repetition to "auxiliary views" wins even for factual recall, with gains widening with scale (reports roughly). Behnia et al. 2510.16096 (Oct 2025) show in a synthetic testbed that contextual diversity delays in-distribution factual accuracy while helping OOD, so the exposure count is not the only knob.

## 2. Gradient-level inefficiency

- **Signal vs noise.** McCandlish et al. 1812.06162: gradient noise scale predicts the critical batch size (CBS). Zhang et al. 2410.21676 (Oct 2024), 85M-1.2B on C4: CBS scales with data size, not model size. 2505.23971 (May 2025) gives a cheap empirical CBS estimator; 2411.00999 (Nov 2024) shows normalization-layer per-example gradients suffice to predict noise scale, so it is measurable on one GPU. Marek et al. 2507.07101 (Jul 2025): with Adam's second-moment half-life held fixed in tokens, batch size 1 trains stably, is more robust to hyperparameters, and matches or beats large batch per FLOP; gradient accumulation is wasteful on one device. Tension with Chang: small batches raise forgetting of injected facts, so the per-FLOP loss view and the fact-retention view disagree.
- **Plasticity.** Dohare et al. (Nature 2024) and 2302.12902 (dormant neurons) come from continual/RL. Applied to LMs: Springer et al. 2503.19206 (Mar 2025) "catastrophic overtraining": OLMo-1B at 3T tokens is >2% worse after instruction tuning than at 2.3T. 2606.24752 (Jun 2026), 5M-314M: plasticity loss appears even in stationary next-token training and its onset scales sublinearly with size, so scale delays but does not prevent it.
- **Optimizers, tokens-to-target.** Claims: Sophia 2305.14342 (May 2023) 2x fewer steps at 125M-1.5B; SOAP 2409.11321 (Sep 2024) >40% fewer iterations vs AdamW at 360M/660M large batch; Muon/Moonlight 2502.16982 (Feb 2025) ~2x compute efficiency; 2505.02222 (May 2025) Muon keeps data efficiency past the AdamW CBS. Independent measurement: Wen et al. 2509.02046 (Sep 2025), with tuned baselines across scales and data ratios, find matrix preconditioners (Muon, SOAP) give 1.4x over AdamW at 0.1B falling to 1.1x at 1.2B; Semenov et al. 2509.01440 (Sep 2025) run a similar 73-page benchmark. The measured multiplier is the number to use; the 2x claims are the caveat.

## 3. Objective inefficiency

- **Token-level waste.** Rho-1 2404.07965 (Apr 2024) tracks per-token loss trajectories during pretraining: 51% of tokens are L→L (learned early, keep paying gradient), 26% H→L (the learning happens here), 12% L→H, 11% H→H (never learned). Selective LM trains on the ~60-70% of tokens a reference model marks as learnable: 1B/7B math reach baseline 5-10x faster, Rho-1-7B matches DeepSeekMath with 15B vs 500B tokens; general-domain 80B-token run gains 6.8% average over 15 tasks. 2601.21571 (Jan 2026) extends token-level filtering to shaping capabilities.
- **Multi-token prediction.** Gloeckle et al. 2404.19737 (Apr 2024): 4-token heads give 13B code models +12% HumanEval, +17% MBPP, at no training cost; the benefit is scale-dependent (hurts below ~100M non-embedding params on induction), persists over multiple epochs (+2.4% MBPP pass@1), and 8-byte prediction solves 67% more MBPP than next-byte. Bachmann & Nagarajan 2403.06963 (Mar 2024) show next-token teacher-forcing fails on lookahead tasks that teacherless training solves.
- **Diffusion LMs under data constraint.** Prabhudesai et al. 2507.15857 (Jul 2025), 7M-2.5B, 25M/50M/100M unique tokens: AR gains stop after ~50 epochs while masked diffusion keeps improving to ~500; effective-epoch constant is ~15-30 for AR vs ~500 for diffusion; critical compute scales as U^~2.17; SciQ 68.7 vs 58.1, Lambada 15.2 vs 10.9. Ni et al. 2511.03276 (Nov 2025): 1B DLM on 1B unique tokens for 480 epochs hits ~56% HellaSwag / ~33% MMLU vs AR ~41/~29; 0.5B unique DLM ≈ 1.5B unique AR (">3x data potential"); 1.7B coder on 10B Python tokens for ~150 epochs crosses over AR; input masking on AR helps but "falls well short." Disagreement: 2510.04071 (Oct 2025) ablates and finds random input masking dominates, and MLP dropout plus weight decay give comparable gains, i.e. stochastic regularization, not any-order modeling. Baseline for repetition: Muennighoff et al. 2305.16264 (May 2023), up to 4 epochs is nearly free for AR, then returns decay to zero.

## 4. Representation / inductive-bias view

- **What 100M tokens buys.** Zhang, Warstadt et al. 2011.04946 (Nov 2020, MiniBERTas 1M-1B words): syntactic and most semantic features are reliably encoded by 10M-100M words; commonsense and downstream NLU keep improving into the billions. BabyLM 2024 findings 2412.05149 (Dec 2024): the winning GPT-BERT 2410.24159 mixes masked and causal objectives, beating either alone at 10M/100M; curriculum and hybrid objectives dominate the leaderboard.
- **Emergence.** Du et al. 2403.15796 (Mar 2024, 1.5B/6B/32B up to 3T tokens plus 28 smaller runs): downstream accuracy is a function of pretraining loss, not size or tokens separately; MMLU/C-Eval/GSM8K sit at chance until loss < ~2.2. Hu et al. 2310.03262 (Oct 2023, PassUntil): with enough samples the "jump" is a metric-resolution artifact; 2.4B code performance predicted to 0.05% before training.
- **Architectural priors.** Recurrent depth 2502.05171 (Feb 2025, 3.5B/800B tokens) matches up to ~50B-param compute by iterating at test time; Ouro 2510.25741 (Oct 2025) 1.4B/2.6B looped models trained on 7.7T tokens match up to 12B models, and the authors attribute this to knowledge manipulation, not capacity. These are parameter and test-time-depth efficiency; no measured token-efficiency multiplier turned up. For program-induction or "neural program" priors in LM pretraining, nothing with token numbers surfaced; 2601.21725 (Jan 2026, procedural pretraining warm-up on abstract data) is the nearest.

## 5. Test-time compute as a substitute for data

- Snell et al. 2408.03314 (Aug 2024): compute-optimal test-time allocation beats best-of-N with 4x less compute; FLOPs-matched, a PaLM-2-S* with test-time compute beats a ~14x larger pretrained model on easy/medium MATH when inference-to-pretraining token ratio R << 1, and loses on hard problems or R >> 1. Wu et al. 2408.00724 (Aug 2024): Llemma-7B with tree search beats Llemma-34B majority voting at every FLOP budget. Brown et al. 2407.21787 (Jul 2024): coverage vs samples is log-linear over four orders of magnitude.
- Trade against pretraining tokens directly: 2604.01411 (Apr 2026), "Train-to-Test" scaling laws jointly over size, tokens, and samples on 8 tasks: once inference is costed, the optimum shifts far into overtraining (smaller model, many more tokens per param), validated by training in the forecast region. TTT: Akyürek et al. 2411.07279 (Nov 2024) get up to 6x on ARC with an 8B model; 2503.11842 (Mar 2025) proves TTT cuts the sample size needed for in-context learning by 3-5x on tabular tasks. Nothing found that shows test-time compute lowering the pretraining tokens needed for a *new* capability, only for extracting a low-probability one.

## Where the tokens go (rough, ordered by measured size)

1. Gradient on already-learned tokens: ~51% of tokens are L→L in Rho-1's trajectory audit (2404.07965).
2. Gradient on never-learnable tokens: ~11% H→H (2404.07965); the irreducible-loss floor.
3. Repetition past the useful range: AR returns fade after ~4 epochs (2305.16264), effective-epoch constant ~15-30 vs ~500 for diffusion (2507.15857).
4. Facts needing 100-1000 exposures for 1-2 bits/param (2404.05405), roughly halved by paraphrase/auxiliary views (2309.14316, 2609.04180), and a 20x junk penalty without domain tags.
5. Forgetting between exposures: power-law decay after each hit, learnability threshold shrinks tens of times at small batch (2406.11813); a single exposure is gone by end of run (2608.19168).
6. Optimizer noise: bounded at 1.1-1.4x by the tuned benchmark (2509.02046), not 2x.
7. Late-run plasticity loss: OLMo-1B worse at 3T than 2.3T after tuning (2503.19206); onset scales sublinearly with size (2606.24752).
8. Objective: next-token vs 4-token gives +12-17% on code only above ~1B (2404.19737); below that the objective is not the bottleneck.

## Cheapest measurements to locate the inefficiency in a 10M-100M-token run on one GPU

- **Token-loss trajectories with a small reference model** (Rho-1 recipe): log per-token loss at 10 checkpoints, bin into L→L/H→L/L→H/H→H. Discriminates wasted gradient (L→L) from unlearnable noise (H→H) from actual learning.
- **Gradient noise scale from norm-layer per-example gradients** (2411.00999) every N steps: tells whether the run is below or above CBS, i.e. optimizer-noise-limited vs data-limited, before touching optimizers.
- **Synthetic-biography fact injection at 10/100/1000 exposures, duplicate vs paraphrase, with probes after each exposure** (2406.11813 + 2309.14316 design): separates storage, extraction, and forgetting, and gives the local exposures-per-bit number.
- **Batch 32 vs 512 at fixed tokens with Adam's token-half-life rule** (2507.07101), scoring both held-out loss and injected-fact retention: discriminates the per-FLOP-loss view from the fact-retention view of small batches.
- **AR vs input-masked AR vs masked diffusion on 25M unique tokens at 1, 10, 50 epochs** (2507.15857 / 2510.04071): tells whether the multi-epoch gain is regularization (masked AR closes it) or objective (only diffusion does).
- **pass@1 vs pass@64 (PassUntil) on a probe task at each checkpoint** (2310.03262): distinguishes a capability that is absent from one that is present at low probability and recoverable by test-time compute instead of more tokens.
