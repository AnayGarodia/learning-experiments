# Literature sweep: why humans need so few tokens, and what small LMs say about each theory (2026-09-09)

## (a) The accounting

**Words heard.** Direct daylong recordings are the best source. Gilkerson et al. 2017 (LENA, 329 families, 3,213 12-hour recordings, 2–48 months) find children hear ~8,000–17,000 adult words per 12-hour day, roughly flat after early infancy ([AJSLP](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2016_AJSLP-15-0169)). That is ~3–6M words/year, so (my arithmetic) ~15–30M by age 5, ~30–60M by 10, ~55–110M by 18. Cross-checks: Hosseini et al. 2024 cite a 9–110M range for a developmentally realistic budget ([Neurobiology of Language](https://direct.mit.edu/nol/article/5/1/43/119156)); Wilcox et al. 2024/25 use "at most 100M by age 12" ([Rycolab](https://rycolab.io/publication/wilcoxal-jml-25/)). Frank 2023 gives a soft *upper bound* of ~1M words/month (~60M by 5, ~2×10⁸ by 20) ([TiCS](https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/abstract/S1364-6613(23)00203-6)). Hart & Risley's 13M/26M/45M-by-age-3 SES figures were extrapolated from one hour per month; LENA revised the SES gap to ~4M by age 4, not 30M, and Sperry et al. 2018 found no gap ([WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/npr-breaking-news/2018-06-01/lets-stop-talking-about-the-30-million-word-gap)).

**What the "100,000x" compares.** Warstadt & Bowman 2022 said ~1000x when models saw ~10¹¹ words ([arXiv 2208.07998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07998)); Llama 3's 15T tokens vs ~10⁸ words makes it ~10⁵. Dwarkesh Patel's 2026 essay uses ~2,000 words/hour → ~2×10⁸ lifetime tokens vs "10s to 100s of trillions," calling it "close to a million-fold" ([dwarkesh.com](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/the-sample-efficiency-black-hole)). Three confounds:
- *Words vs tokens*: ×~1.3.
- *Text vs multimodal*: LeCun's 16,000 waking hours × 2×10⁶ optic-nerve fibres × 10 B/s ≈ 10¹⁵ bytes by age 4, "50x more than the biggest LLM" ([X](https://x.com/ylecun/status/1750614681209983231)); Knoche's more careful accounting gives ~300 TB of visual data over 12 years, ~15x a text corpus, not orders of magnitude ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tmWxDGnuNdaHFDyjf/dissolving-the-deep-learning-sample-efficiency-gap-1)).
- *Compute per token* (my derivation, whole-brain): 10¹⁴–10¹⁵ FLOP/s ÷ (~2,000 words/hr ÷ 3,600 s) ≈ 2×10¹⁴–2×10¹⁵ FLOP per word heard, versus 6N ≈ 4×10¹¹ (70B) to 2.4×10¹² (405B) FLOP per training token. A human spends ~10²–10³ *more* compute per token. Lifetime ≈ 10¹⁵ × 5×10⁸ awake seconds ≈ 5×10²³ FLOP, within ~2 OOM of a frontier run. Norman Mu adds the evolutionary ledger: 10⁴ generations × 10⁸ tokens ≈ 2T tokens, Llama-2 scale ([normanmu.com](https://www.normanmu.com/2025/02/14/data-inefficiency-llms.html)).

Net: the token gap is real (~10⁴–10⁵ on text), but the *compute-per-token* gap runs the other way, and roughly half the bytes gap disappears when multimodality is counted.

## (b) Theories and their model-side tests

**(1) Innate priors / wrong architecture.** *For:* McCoy & Griffiths distil a Bayesian prior into a network via MAML; standard nets need ~10x the examples to match it ([Nat. Comms 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59957-y)). LAD-inspired pre-pretraining on MP-STRUCT (500 steps) matches k-Shuffle Dyck token efficiency and adds human-like resistance to impossible languages ([2605.16758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16758)). "Model Connectomes": an evolved sparse init trained on 100M tokens is "better or on par" on NLP and brain alignment ([2504.21047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21047)). Yedetore et al. 2023: LSTMs/transformers on CHILDES-scale data prefer the linear over the hierarchical rule ([2301.11462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11462)). *Against:* PoSHbench finds cognitively motivated biases raise broad syntax but "do not consistently translate to PoS-relevant generalization" ([2602.09992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09992)); Wilcox, Futrell & Levy show filler-gap constraints emerge from plain LMs ([LI 2024](https://direct.mit.edu/ling/article/doi/10.1162/ling_a_00491/113304)). Testable at small scale: yes, and it is.

**(2) Multimodal grounding.** *For:* Vong et al. 2024 learn word-referent maps from 61 hours of one child's video (61.6% on 22 concepts) ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1374)); replicated across three SAYCam children ([2507.14749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14749)). *Against:* Amariucai & Warstadt: multimodal pretraining "does not harm ... but does not consistently help" text benchmarks ([2402.17936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17936)); BabyLM 2024 multimodal submissions did not reliably beat text-only ([2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1)); BabyLM 2025 multimodal track: 3 teams, 8 submissions, none beat baselines, no winner ([BabyLM 2026 CfP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20092)). Grounding buys reference, not syntax efficiency, so far.

**(3) Interaction / active learning.** *For:* TnD (student trials + teacher demonstrations) speeds word acquisition (nAoA 2.10 vs 2.93) but SuperGLUE is flat ([2405.13828](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13828)); RL on caregiver-feedback reward models: structural alignment helps grammaticality most, semantic/affective feedback not at all ([2609.08576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08576)); BabyLM 2025 interaction track (Llama-3.1-8B teacher) beat baselines ([findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28/)). *Against:* "Dialogue is not enough": dialogue-only 135M model gets 56.05 BLiMP vs 72.16 baseline, DPO adds ~4% on dialogue pairs, PPO "mixed to adversarial," nothing improves formal benchmarks ([2510.20358](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20358)). Gap: I found no LM result where the *learner* selects its own training data; all "interaction" work is teacher- or curriculum-selected.

**(4) Curriculum and child-directed speech.** Mixed by construction. *For:* curriculum cuts steps to baseline by 18–45% in early/mid training ([2506.11300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11300)); CDS-trained 10M models produce grammatical completions much earlier (92.8% vs 44.1% on CHILDES frames) ([2606.01045](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01045v1)). *Against:* same paper, BLiMP 63.7 (CHILDES) vs 68.0 (FineWeb-edu); CDS "does not consistently boost syntax" ([2505.23689](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23689)); CLIMB got the BabyLM 2023 negative-result award and 41.9% of teams tried curriculum without consistent gains ([2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1)). The metric decides the answer.

**(5) Replay / consolidation / sleep.** *For:* up to 4 epochs of repeated data is free at fixed compute ([Muennighoff 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264)); ELC-BERT won BabyLM 2023 with 450–2000 epochs, seeing as many samples as BERT ([2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08165v1)); Zhao, Duan & Cai argue inner speech and sleep replay are the "missing half" of input ([Open Mind 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12506926/)); "Do LMs Need Sleep?" offline recurrence improves online inference on synthetic tasks ([2605.26099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26099)). *Against:* repetition returns decay to zero beyond ~4 epochs; the 2025 challenge capped FLOPs and saw "no complete correlation between training FLOPs and performance." This is the compute-per-token theory in disguise, and it is cheap to test.

**(6) Meta-learning / learning to learn.** *For:* Minnow (meta in-context word learning) trained from scratch on CHILDES (5.8M tokens) gets 72% 4-way novel-word accuracy, ≈ Llama-3 8B's 71% ([2502.14791](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14791v1)); MAML+SMLMT interleaved in pretraining reaches target loss up to 1.6x sooner, +2–3 pt NER ([2508.02189](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02189)); MLC matches humans on few-shot compositional instruction learning and beats GPT-4 ([Nature 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06668-3)). *Against:* 2508.02189 degrades Paloma perplexity; MLC is trained on a synthetic episode stream, not natural data.

**(7) Compression / world models.** *For:* BPL reaches human-level one-shot Omniglot ([Science 2015](https://science.sciencemag.org/content/350/6266/1332)); Yang & Piantadosi learn 56 formal languages from few examples ([PNAS 2022](https://www.pnas.org/content/119/5/e2021865119)); Colas et al. show linguistic advice plus executable world models speeds learning across 10 games ([ICLR 2026, 2509.00074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00074)); children invent tool uses LLMs cannot ([Yiu, Kosoy & Gopnik 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17456916231201401)). *Against:* the JEPA line is a bandwidth argument with no language sample-efficiency number ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09985)); no small-LM test exists.

**(8) The gap is (mostly) an illusion.** *For:* 10–100M words suffice for syntax and most semantics ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.90/)); 100M-word models predict brain responses about as well as full-scale ones ([Hosseini 2024](https://direct.mit.edu/nol/article/5/1/43/119156)); GPT-2-small fits reading times better than large models ([Wilcox 2025](https://rycolab.io/publication/wilcoxal-jml-25/)); single-child input (212–240k tokens) yields 65–70% Zorro across six architectures ([2402.07899](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07899)); the compute-per-token arithmetic in (a); Mu's evolutionary ledger. *Against:* what remains after syntax (commonsense, reasoning, innovation) is exactly where children win, and Dwarkesh's Chinchilla point: infinite parameters only cut data ~10x.

## (c) 2025–2026 position papers and labs

- **Flapping Airplanes** (launched 28 Jan 2026, $180M seed, Ben & Asher Spector, Aidan Smith): target "1000x wins in data efficiency"; named directions are memorization-vs-reasoning trade-offs, few-example post-training adaptability, brain-as-existence-proof but not neuromorphic copying. No published results or preprints as of this sweep ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/16/flapping-airplanes-on-the-future-of-ai-we-want-to-try-really-radically-different-things/), [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/partnering-with-flapping-airplanes)).
- **Dwarkesh Patel 2026**: "million-x" gap, data as the real driver; defers mechanisms to a future post.
- **"Why AI systems don't learn"** ([2603.15381](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15381v1)): System A (observational), B (action), M (meta-control: attention biases, critical periods, sleep consolidation). Position paper, no results.
- **Knoche 2025**: gap dissolves into priors + multimodal + model-based RL + replay; most fixes cost *more* compute per example.
- **BabyLM 2026** goes multilingual and keeps the compute cap ([2602.20092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20092)).
